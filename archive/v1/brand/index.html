Dump it. Find it.

Text yourself, but it remembers. This is the guide to how Dumpster looks, sounds and behaves, made by Range Of View Studios.

Scraps, resident raccoon
01 / The idea

Nobody organizes. Everybody dumps.

Mom texts an address, you text it to yourself, and it is gone forever. Dumpster is that same habit with a memory. You toss anything into one thread, quietly it gets sorted into Piles, and later you just ask for it in plain language. Zero effort in, organized out.

The brand in one line

Loud on the outside, calm on the inside.

The look is hazard-sign loud so it feels like play. The behavior is quiet and dependable so it feels like trust.

02b / Logo options

Logo options

Six directions for the mark and two wordmark treatments, for the client to choose from. The current logo comes first for comparison. Each option is hand-built SVG, tested at 29px, and each card shows the mark on yellow and ink, the app icon at 180, 60 and 29px, and a lockup. Files live in assets/logo-options/.

v1

Current (v1)

The baseline the options are measured against. It has the right character, but it is a small illustration instead of a mark.

Strengths
  • Scraps is charming and instantly on brand.
  • Bold color and line weight.
Watch-outs
  • The raccoon in a slanted bin can read as a shopping cart at small sizes.
  • The hard white offset shadow on the wordmark looks like a glitch on yellow and pink (see the pink panel).
A

Chat Bin

A speech bubble is the lid of the bin. One shape says chat, the other says dump, and the three typing dots go straight in.

Strengths
  • Explains the product in one glance: chat in, bin out.
  • Symmetrical and geometric, and the lid is easy to animate open and shut.
Watch-outs
  • Two stacked shapes get busy under 40px.
  • The wide body can still drift toward a basket if the ribs get too many.
B

Bubble Dumpster

The whole mark is a chat bubble dressed as a dumpster: a lid band, ribs, wheels for feet and the tail doing double duty as a kickstand.

Strengths
  • One bold silhouette, so it holds together as a single shape at small sizes.
  • The tail gives the bin a direction and a bit of attitude.
Watch-outs
  • At 29px it reads bin first and chat second.
  • Wheels and tail compete at the bottom edge; keep them simple.
C

Mask

Just Scraps' mask band and two eyes inside a grey face. A character instead of a scene, so it survives as a favicon, a notification badge and a 16px tab.

Strengths
  • Clearest of the six at 16 to 29px.
  • Ownable, and it shares its DNA with the mascot we already have.
Watch-outs
  • No bin or chat cue on its own; the idea lives in the name beside it.
  • A raccoon face is a common animal mark, and the ink mask melts into ink backgrounds (the grey face carries it).
D

d monogram

A chunky lowercase d. The bowl is the bin and the ascender is the post the lid is hinged to, propped open in pink.

Strengths
  • Works as a single letter and in one color.
  • The open lid gives motion a clear moment to play.
Watch-outs
  • Weakest at 29px: the lid shrinks to a speck and the d starts to look like a boxy block.
  • Needs another round of refinement to feel as crafted as the others.
E

Pile stack

Three folder-tab cards in pile colors, stacked so the pile also reads as a bin. It is the Piles screen, made into a logo.

Strengths
  • Ties straight to the product: this is what Dumpster does with your stuff.
  • Staggered tabs and pile colors are distinctive and cheerful.
Watch-outs
  • Reads as files and folders before it reads as a dumpster.
  • Tabs go thin at 29px, and it needs a one-color version for print.
F

Sticker badge

Not for small sizes

A round stamp with Scraps peeking over a bin, ringed by DUMPSTER, DUMP IT and FIND IT. Made for stickers, merch and social avatars, not for the app icon.

Strengths
  • Carries the tagline and the personality in one object.
  • The ring type is outlined, so it needs no font.
Watch-outs
  • The ring text is unreadable below about 120px. That is by design; do not use it as an app icon.
  • Too much detail for one-color or embossed uses.

Wordmark treatments

Both keep the existing geometric letter construction, so no font is needed. They replace the white shadow that clashes with yellow and pink.

W1: ink outline, ink hard shadow

w1-wordmark.svg Cream letters, ink outline, ink offset shadow. The classic neo-brutal sticker. On ink, w1-wordmark-on-ink.svg swaps the shadow to yellow so it stays visible.

W2: the u carries a bin

w2-wordmark.svg Flat ink letters. The u becomes the bin: mint inside, pink lid propped on its edge. On ink, w2-wordmark-on-ink.svg switches the letters to cream.

Our recommendation

Our pick

C, Mask, with the W2 wordmark

After rendering all six at 29px, the mask is the only one that is unmistakable at a glance. It is a character instead of a scene, so there is nothing to clutter, and it is ours: nobody else has Scraps. The v1 problem (the cart read) disappears because there is no bin to misread.

Pair it with W2, where the u is a tiny bin, so the dumpster idea is carried by the name next to the mark. B (Bubble Dumpster) is the runner-up if the client wants the chat and bin idea inside the icon itself. Use A's bubble lid as an illustration motif, E for the Piles screen and F for stickers and merch.

03 / Meet Scraps

Meet Scraps

A trash panda who digs through everything so you do not have to. Cute but graphic: grey body, a darker mask band, white eye highlights, a ringed tail and small hands. Same line weight in every pose.

Peek

Default. App icon, splash, small spots.

Dig

Searching and loading. "Scraps is sorting."

Hold

Save confirmations and success moments.

Sleep

Empty states. "Nothing here yet. Scraps is bored."

Wave

Onboarding and first run.

Anatomy

Body #A9A9A9 Mask band #3E3E3E White eye highlights Cream muzzle + belly Ringed tail, 5 to 6 rings Small dark hands Ink line 3px at 120px

Usage rules

  1. One Scraps per screen, and never in the way of the content. He supports, he does not star.
  2. Pose follows meaning. Peek is the default. Do not use Wave for an error or Sleep for a success.
  3. Sit him on a flat fill: yellow, cream or a pile color. Never on photos, patterns or gradients.
  4. Do not recolor the body. The grey is what makes him read against every pile accent.
  5. Minimum 64px tall in product. Below that, use the app icon instead.
  6. He never talks in speech bubbles or uses emoji. Copy sits next to him, in the product voice.

Contrast notes

  • RuleAll text on accent fills is ink. White on pink (2.9:1), violet (4.2:1) and every other accent fails AA, so white is not used on any pile color.
  • VioletInk on violet measures 4.49:1, a hair under AA for body text. Use it for 18px bold labels, icons and tabs, and put small text on cream.
  • SemanticSuccess is mint, danger is #FF4D4D. Ink on danger is 5.8:1. Never signal state by color alone: pair with an icon or word.
  • On yellowCream on yellow is 1.4:1, so separation always comes from the ink border and shadow, not from the fill change.

09 / Voice and tone

Voice and tone

Playful with a light touch. Plain and clear almost everywhere, a little cheeky in a few chosen places.

Grounded

Say what happened and what is next. Substance over noise, no hype, no exclamation pile-ups.

Warm

A helpful friend who remembers things for you. Never cold, never a robot, never a scold.

Cheeky

A raised eyebrow, not a stand-up set. One light touch per screen, at the most.

Pun rules

  • Puns live on verbs and nav only. Toss it, Piles, Dig, Scraps. That is the whole list.
  • Key moments only: saving, searching, empty states, onboarding.
  • Errors, settings, legal, money: plain. Never joke when something went wrong.
